## 🔍 Part 1: From Lazy to Precise

**Lazy prompt:** *"Answer Kim's mail."* This typically produces a friendly mail of 100 words or more: a greeting, a paragraph per topic, and phrases like *"we're well on track for PI Planning"* that no file supports.

**Precise prompt:** *"Answer Kim's mail from inbox.md in one sentence of at most 40 words …"* The answer:

> Yes: delay feed v2 runs in the test environment at 22 seconds (goal: below 30); our biggest blocker is the provider's test data, late for the third sprint in a row, so the peak-hour load test (SIG-145) is still open.

| Principle | Where it shows in the precise prompt |
|---|---|
| 1 · Be concise | "one sentence of at most 40 words" |
| 2 · Be clear | Two exact questions: goal reached? Biggest blocker? |
| 3 · Context and structure | "Use only sprint-notes.md" |
| 4 · Break down complex tasks | One prompt per mail |
| 5 · Specify the output | Length, form and the item ID |
| 6 · Reflect and adapt | When an answer misses, sharpen the prompt and run it again |
| 7 · Combine the principles | All of the above in one prompt |

> ⚠️ **Watch out:** Kiro may answer Kim with "no", because the PI focus says **15 seconds** and the feed runs at 22. But the Sprint 14 goal said **below 30 seconds**, so the honest answer is **yes**. Two targets, two different questions.

## 🛡️ Part 2: Two Sensitive Replies

### 🏛️ Robin: Only What Is True Today

Delete anything Kiro adds that you can't back up, for example *"all data is encrypted"*, *"fully GDPR-compliant"* or *"we will send you our AI rules by Friday"*. Once a promise is in your reply, it is <span style="color:#EC0016;font-weight:700">your promise</span>.

> 💡 **Did you notice?** Robin's own mail went into Kiro, too. An honest answer says so.

### ⚖️ Sascha: Two Tones, One Choice

| | 😌 Calm and reassuring | ⚡ Direct and urgent |
|---|---|---|
| **Strength** | Easy to read and causes no panic | Names the risk, the impact and the ask |
| **Typical traps** | Promises nobody made, such as *"on track"*, *"resolved next sprint"* or *"no impact on the PI"*, and downplaying, such as *"a minor issue"* | Exaggeration, such as *"critical"* or *"the PI is in danger"*, and invented figures such as cost estimates |

The sample chooses the **direct** version: the steering committee has to act, and it can only act on a clear risk and a clear ask. The calm tone is not the problem; the unsupported promises are. A calm version that sticks to the facts is a good choice, too.

## 💡 What to Take Away

1. **Precise beats long:** a short prompt with context and a defined output saves you the rework.
2. **A role changes the voice, not the facts.** The facts still come from your files.
3. **Tone is your choice,** and so is every promise in your text.
