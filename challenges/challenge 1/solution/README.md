## 🔍 Part 1: Did Kiro Invent Anything?

Check your summary for these typical mistakes. If you find one, your check did its job:

- <span style="color:#EC0016;font-weight:700">Invented details:</span> real names for Member A to F, story points, a velocity, or a date for PI Planning. None of these are in the files.
- **Wrong numbers:** for example "5 of 7 items done" instead of 4 of 7.
- **Open questions turned into facts:** for example "Concourse owns the platform-change events". In the files, this is still open.
- **Mixed-up targets:** the sprint goal says "below 30 seconds", the PI focus says "15 seconds". A summary that treats them as one target is wrong.

## 🧪 Part 2: Before and After

**Test question:** *"Write a two-sentence welcome message for a new member of our team."*

| | Example answer |
|---|---|
| ❌ **Without** team steering file | *"Welcome to the team! We're thrilled to have you on board and look forward to achieving great things together."* |
| ✅ **With** team steering file | *"Welcome to Team Signal! We deliver the real-time train data behind SmartTransfer, and our Daily at 09:15 is the best place to meet everyone."* |
| ✅ **Also a good outcome** | Kiro first asks: *"What is the new team member's name and role?"* That is your rule "ask before you answer" at work. |

Your wording will differ. What matters is the **difference** between your two answers.

## 🗣️ What Changed? Two Good Explanations

- *"Without the team steering file, the message was generic. With it, Kiro named our team, our work and our Daily, in our tone."*
- *"With the steering file, Kiro asked for the new member's name first, because our rule tells it to ask when something is unclear."*

## 💡 What to Take Away

1. **Build the context first:** let Kiro read the files before you ask.
2. **Check every statement** against the source. Kiro sounds just as sure when it is wrong.
3. **A short, accurate steering file** changes every answer that follows.
