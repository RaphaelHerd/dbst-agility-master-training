## 🔍 What the Critic Found

| Quote from the draft | Problem | Better |
|---|---|---|
| "As one of the more experienced colleagues, D sometimes struggles to keep up with new tools" | **Coded language:** links experience, which many readers hear as age, to a stereotype. No example | A concrete situation, or leave it out |
| "prefers the old ways of working" | **No evidence:** a label, not an observation | An example, or leave it out |
| "a bit too emotional in discussions" | **Coded language:** judges personality. Labels like this are applied unevenly to different people | Describe the behavior and its effect in one situation |
| "not a natural leader" | **A fixed trait,** nothing anyone can observe or develop | A development step, if D wants one |
| "often quiet, so I'm not sure D is ambitious enough" | **Two assumptions:** being quiet says nothing about ambition | Ask D about their goals in the talk |
| "The pipeline outage last week shows that D is not always reliable" | **Recency bias:** one recent event is judged as a pattern. The retro notes describe the red pipeline as a team problem: "nobody felt responsible" | Leave it out, or describe D's own part with evidence |
| "Reviews from D are often late" | **No evidence:** which reviews, how late, with what effect? | A specific example |
| "On the positive side, D did the data mapping for regional trains." | **Imbalance:** one line of strengths after five lines of criticism | Describe the contribution and its effect |
| "Overall: meets expectations" | **A rating without criteria** in an input draft | Leave ratings to the development talk |
