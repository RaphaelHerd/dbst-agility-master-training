## 🎯 What a Good Result Looks Like

| Jury criterion | Your pack is good when … |
|---|---|
| **One story** | The page, the slides and the sentence say the same thing, in the same order |
| **Numbers** | Every number traces back to a source, and no outdated number survives |
| **Decisions** | The decisions come first, and each one is a clear question with a reason |
| **Ten minutes** | Three slides, one message each, no ticket IDs |
| **Trust** | AI label, review gate passed, no names of team members |

## 🧭 Part 1: The Trap in the Sources

`train-status.md` still contains **Tuesday's forecast**: 12 weeks with 85 % confidence. But since today, Team Signal has two people fewer, and the rollout moves to the following PI.

> ⚠️ A pack that says *"live in 12 weeks"* on one page and *"moves to the following PI"* on the next tells two stories. The skeleton prompt asks Kiro to **mark where sources disagree**. That's where the trap shows up.

## 🖋️ Part 2: One Story, Three Formats

| Format | What it does | Sample |
|---|---|---|
| **One-pager** | Decisions first, then status, forecast, priorities and risks | Decisions, a team table, one sentence per topic |
| **Slides** | One message per slide, as the headline | "Ready, with one change" · "Clear priorities, two risks" · "Three decisions" |
| **Sentence** | Status, change and ask in one breath | 29 words |

> 💡 **Write the story once, then translate it.** Three separate prompts produce three different stories; one skeleton keeps them aligned.

## 🏆 Part 3: The Consistency Check

## 💡 What to Take Away

1. **One skeleton, three formats.** The story comes first, the formats follow.
2. **Check what's still true.** A week of results contains numbers that expired along the way.
3. **Your review gate is your quality promise,** even, and especially, under time pressure.
